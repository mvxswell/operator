Prioritization Skill Test

Practice choosing what happens first when several requests compete for the same time. The scenarios reward attention to deadlines, dependencies, and consequences instead of the loudest request or the easiest task.

Start this test

What this set tests

  • Distinguish an urgent consequence from an urgent-sounding request.
  • Sequence work around dependencies and scarce attention.
  • Name which work should wait when capacity is limited.

How to approach the scenarios

  1. Identify the consequence of delaying each option.
  2. Check for tasks that unlock or block the rest of the work.
  3. Choose a sequence, not a wish that everything can happen at once.

A sample from the question bank

It is 7:15am, your busiest hour. The espresso machine just stopped working, you are low on napkins, and this week's Instagram post is not written.

Feedback and progress

Difficulty adapts as you answer. The final score combines accuracy, difficulty handled, and decision speed; accuracy carries the most weight. These are practice scenarios, not a professional certification or a validated hiring assessment. Use the feedback to examine your reasoning. The information needed to answer is in the case; real operating decisions may require facts and advice beyond a short exercise.
